# Direction and appearance

## Story design (minimum)

Before previs, the project needs an approved story file (`story-vNNN.json`, the `story-design` skill's format) with at least:

- `setup`: `premise`, `tone`, `avoid`, `lead` and `setting`, settled before any plot; `hook` (what is funny or surprising) is optional. Draft them yourself and let the user edit; offer alternatives for `tone` only.
- `purpose`: one sentence the viewer should take away.
- `target_action`: the viewer action (save, share, follow, book…), its `cue` (caption, line, end card) and the `beat` where it lands, at or after the payoff.
- `structure`: `id` and `why` linking purpose and action to the structure.
- `beats`: contiguous timed beats from 0 s, each with a visible `event`, `tension` 0–5 and `serves` (`purpose` or `target_action`).

Conflict only when the structure needs it. When it does, an antagonist must act, not just appear: name who blocks and how in each obstacle beat, make the hero pay a visible cost, and let tension peak right before the payoff. Shots, events and entity counts in previs derive from these beats; a previs simplification that removes an obstacle beat changes the story and reopens story approval.

## Reference-led route

Use when the user wants a reference video's composition, action and timing reproduced, with new characters, products or setting. An approved reference breakdown takes the story design's place; do not invent a purpose, target action or tension curve for it.

1. Preserve the footage as a local file in `inputs/`. With only a link or description, say what cannot be observed: timing cannot be matched without frames. A screen recording is not the piece: cut it to the piece itself, resample to a constant frame rate and keep the recording beside it. Its cut times wander by a frame or two and interface elements cover part of the picture, so say so in the source record; it serves a `format` match, not a `shot` match.
2. From the project directory run `media.py cuts inputs/reference.mp4 reference/v001`. It writes `reference-draft.json`, start/mid/end `stills` and a `clip` per detected shot. Detection is a proposal: inspect the stills and fix missed or false cuts (dissolves, flashes, whip pans) by editing shot boundaries. One source shot is one reference shot. Split it only when it is longer than the profile's `max_duration_s`: mark the later part `continues_previous: true` and give a `split_reason`; the gate rejects a split whose parts would fit one take. The draft also lists `candidates`: scene changes scored between `--low` and the threshold. Cuts between similar pictures and cuts a few frames apart land there or go unseen. A candidate with `from` and `to` stands for a run of them (one shaky shot, usually): the stretch to look at is the whole run, because a real cut can sit inside it. For each candidate, and for every shot shorter than half a second, extract every frame of that stretch (`media.py extract … --every 1/FPS`) and look at it; then split the shot or write why it is not a cut in the candidate's `decision`. Count the shots again after this pass and keep boundaries as the tool wrote them: times retyped in tenths of a second moved ten cuts by two to three frames in one production.
3. Measure before describing the camera: at three or more times per shot, read the subject's head width and a background figure's face width (when focus blurs the background, the distance between two fixed background points) in pixels off the frames and record them as `camera_evidence` (`{t, subject_px, background_px}`; the gate needs two samples, except when `match` is `format`: its cameras are designed new, so the reference's are only described). The subject shrinking while the background holds means the subject moves away; both shrinking together means a pull-back or zoom-out; the `camera` text must agree with the numbers. Then per shot fill `camera` (shot size, angle, height, lens feel, movement, subject placement) and `action`. Time each action change as an `events` entry `{t, event}` in source seconds, read from the shot clip's frames at the source frame rate (`media.py extract reference/v001/s02.mp4 review/s02-frames --every 1/FPS` as a decimal); an event at a cut belongs to the next shot. For every figure that changes place, write the shot's `travel` entry for its role: where it ends up, the route it takes and who stands beside it ("walks straight to the empty place right of the front-row boy"); a route left unwritten gets invented in previs. For every role with five or more members, measure how far apart they change state (`media.py spread`) and note it for the shot's `group_motion`. Add a top-level `changes` list: what is replaced (performer → mascot, product, setting) and what stays. Before describing shots, add the same top-level `setup` block a story design has (the adaptation's premise, tone, what to avoid, lead and setting; a `hook` only if it has one): an adaptation inherits the source's mood unless `tone` and `avoid` say otherwise, and an eerie source reproduced faithfully gives an eerie parody.
4. Record whose footage it is and how closely you follow it, before describing shots. `source.rights` is `own` or `third_party`. Third-party footage is watched and measured, never shown: no frame of it reaches the edit, an asset or a keyframe, and it goes to a provider as a motion reference only after the user agrees, quoted in `source.upload_agreed` (the gate checks job inputs). `match` is `format` (pace, cut lengths, caption style, viewpoint: the shots themselves are new) or `shot` (composition and action per shot as well). With `format`, list in the review every shot whose composition or action still reproduces the reference's, as a decision for the user: a cut-for-cut mapping drifts toward `shot` without anyone choosing it. The shots of a `format` match are new, so each reference shot also carries a `plan`: `source` (`generated`, `footage` for a clip of your own used as it is, `reuse` for a frame taken from another shot), `what` (the footage clip and stretch, or one line on the new shot: size, angle, who does what) and `caption`. The table of plans is what the user approves in place of a story; a `footage` or `reuse` shot needs no previs.
5. Save as `reference-v001.json`; run `gate.py reference reference-v001.json --max-duration <the profile's max_duration_s>` until it prints `OK`. Review with the user as a table (id, start–end, frames at the output fps, camera, action, events) with each shot's stills, then record `reference` in state.
6. Previs shot ids equal reference shot ids. Each shot's frames = round(end × fps) − round(start × fps) at the output fps, so rounding never accumulates; key each event at frame round(t × fps) − round(start × fps) + 1 of its shot. `gate.py ready` enforces shot frames. A shot that intentionally differs (dropped, merged, retimed, reframed for another aspect ratio) gets a `decision` in a new reference version and needs re-approval.
7. Review previs with `media.py compare inputs/reference.mp4 PREVIS.mp4 review/ref-vs-previs.mp4` (synced side by side) plus the per-cut reference ↔ previs sheet below. When the delivery aspect ratio differs from the source, first make the comparison clip with `media.py reframe` (a crop window that follows the subject) and record the crop width and what the new frame gains and loses as a `decision`.
8. A turn, change of direction or stop that a figure makes in previs and the reference does not list is an added action: it will appear in the take. Put it to the user as a decision and record it in the shot's `unlisted_motion` (`{t, decision}`), or remove it. `gate.py ready` reads these moves off the scene file.

## Footage-led production

Use when the location, product or service was really filmed and people or framings are generated on top: a partner's shop filmed on a phone, with a generated guest. The real footage is the authority on what the place looks like; the checkpoints stay the same.

- Clearance first. Before any frame is used, table every clip in `brief.md`: who is in it and when (hands count), what third-party marks are visible (characters, logos, artwork, other brands) and which stretch or crop is clear. Only clear stretches go to the edit. A guest or passer-by in the footage is never used, hands included, and never passed to a generator. Staff may appear when the owner has given consent for it; record the consent, or mark the clip as pending and settle it before publication.
- Set references are people-free. Cut a set reference from a clear frame, or remove the person from a copy, before binding it as a `set` asset: a reference image that shows a person hands their tattoo, hair and hands to the output.
- Facts come from the footage, as pictures. List in `brief.md` what must match the real place (colour of the water, staff clothing, tools, the order of a treatment) with the frame that shows each. Give the generator that frame or a crop of it as a reference; a colour or garment described only in words is invented again, and a correction in words overshoots.
- When no people-free frame of a prop or a room exists (a mask is only ever on a face, a mitt only on a back), crop to the material alone and give shape, size and coverage in words; removing the person from a copy is an image generation and needs the budget like any other.
- Casting and brand preferences live in `brief.md`, not here.
- A generated person in a real place is not a visitor: no first-person or testimonial wording in captions, and the place's owner agrees before publication. Record both in `brief.md`.

## Intake routes

| Input | Action |
|---|---|
| Reference footage | To reproduce it, follow the reference-led route. As loose inspiration: probe it, extract frames around cuts and action changes, observe camera, composition, actions and timing; list unavailable evidence. |
| Idea or script | Develop a concrete beginning, change, consequence and ending; write dialogue and shot actions. Label creative proposals. |
| Approved storyboard/previs | Reuse the approved revision; fill only missing technical or production details. |

A storyboard panel may be a start, middle or end of one shot; do not equate panel count with generation calls. Preserve the user's chosen medium, tone and audience. Channel strategy and trend research are separate assignments.

Record for each shot: ID, duration in frames, subject IDs, setting, camera, start state, action, end state, required events with target frame windows, dialogue, sound cues and continuity constraints. Put voice timing into previs: measure scratch/approved voice duration and recheck timing when it changes. Scratch voice can be local or user-provided; paid scratch work needs the budget first.

## Shot design

Applies when shots are designed from an idea, a script or story beats. On the reference-led route the reference decides camera and cuts: skip this section.

- Beats before shots: list what the viewer must learn, one fact per line, from the story beats' `event`s. Group consecutive facts into shots. A fact no shot shows is a defect. Fewer shots is the default: every cut costs the viewer reading time, and inside one location it is also a continuity risk (sequence takes, generation.md).
- Neutral camera first: the subject's eye height, lens 40 mm, static. With two figures, a height between their eyes. Every departure (low, high, wide, long, a move) carries its reason in the shot's camera record ("low: he looms over her"); one without a reason goes back to neutral.
- One thing to find after each cut: name the shot's focal subject, list it first in `audit.subjects` and keep competing subjects and occluders off it in the first frame.
- Shot size sets the distance. Visible height = subject height × 2 (wide), 1.15 (full), 0.6 (medium), 0.4 (medium close-up) or 0.25 (close-up). Camera distance = visible height × lens ÷ sensor height, where sensor height is 36 mm for a frame taller than wide and 36 × height ÷ width otherwise (20.25 mm at 16:9). A lens change at the same size moves the camera: d2 = d1 × f2 ÷ f1.
- Action line: when two figures face or address each other, every camera in that location stays on one side of the line through them, so each keeps its side of the screen. Declare the pair as `audit.line` in each of those shots. A crossing on purpose is a decision for the user; record it and set `line_cross_ok`.
- Screen direction: a figure on a journey keeps travelling the same way across the screen from shot to shot, and the way back reverses it. Declare it as `audit.screen_direction` in each shot of the journey.
- Move the camera only when the move is the point of the shot (a reveal, a follow, a push onto the detail that matters).

## Previs review

Use simple geometry sufficient to expose composition, scale, occlusion, movement and contact. A jump requires takeoff, airborne arc and landing; rigid sliding blocks may be inadequate. Add proxy parts or custom Blender logic when needed. The bundled renderer supports primitive/capsule transforms, camera paths, target tracking and lens keys, with sampled geometry audits. It is not a full rigging system. Preserve its limitations in the review notes.

Which shots get a previs: every shot where something has to be placed relative to something else. That is a figure standing, sitting or moving in a room, two subjects whose positions matter to each other, a camera that moves, and any shot that shows a character's back or side (the appearance sheets are decided from it). A close insert or a point-of-view detail with one subject and nothing to block (a hand opening a card, mitts on a table, a foot entering water) may skip it: boxes and cylinders filling the frame say less than one sentence, and fitting a proxy hand costs more than it tells. Record the reason in the shot's `no_previs`, give its `frames`, and write its framing in words (size, angle, what enters from where); it is reviewed at the keyframe instead. On the reference-led route a reference shot that is not generated at all says so in its `plan` (`footage` or `reuse`). A shot without previs still names what is in its frame in `object_roles` (or `{}`): the asset, prop and continuity checks hang on it.

Proxy minimums:

- Story-critical props are blocked with their supporting structure, not as a bare plank or floating shape (a slide is chute + ladder/support + exit lip).
- Poses that change meaning are represented: a sleeping passenger is seated or reclined, not a standing capsule.
- Entity counts match the story beats (a swarm is several proxies, not one).
- In the delivery aspect ratio, no ground edge or empty void is visible inside the frame unless intended.
- Every proxy object id is mapped in the shot's `object_roles` to a role or to `null` (set dressing without its own asset).
- A prop's whole path is blocked and named in the shot's `props`: where it comes from (`origin`), who holds it (`holder`) and where it ends (`exit`). Hiding a proxy inside another proxy until it is needed is a staging trick, so record the real source (a pocket, a table) there. A prop with no stated source appears from nowhere in the generated take. Also record its `count` (how many exist in the whole take) and its `states`: `{t, where}` for each place it is held, from the first frame ("in her mouth, ball hidden, only the stick shows" → "between two fingers at her chest" → "in her mouth"). A touch that leaves the prop where it is (pinching a stick that stays in the mouth) is not a state; drop it or put it to the user as a risk, because a take renders it as a second prop in the hand.

Floor plan: when two or more shots share a location that holds two or more figures, or a figure travels more than a metre in it, render one plan for those shots (`render_previs.py … --plan s01,s02,s03`) before the first full render. Look for cameras on both sides of an action line, a figure whose path jumps between shots, and a camera standing inside a set piece. Show the plan with the previs review, and use it when preparing set images so the background fits the layout. Skip it for a location with one figure that stays put, a location seen in one shot, and on the reference-led route.

Revise on stills, render once. While the blocking is still changing, run `render_previs.py --audit-only`, then render only the event frames (`--frames`) and compare them with the reference or storyboard (`media.py pair --times`). Render the full animation once, after the stills match. Drafts you revise alone go to `previs/d01`, `previs/d02`…; only a render shown for review is numbered `v001`, `v002`…, so the first version the user sees is v001. Nothing is overwritten. Share the first comparison sheet as soon as it exists, with the fixes you plan: it is information, not an approval request. When a background render starts, say how long it is expected to take.

Prepare the animatic plus key frames at cuts and required events, with shot IDs and frame numbers in the review description. Inspect the actual render before asking the user to approve. Save approval evidence and revision/hash.

Reference comparison: when a storyboard, reference footage or earlier approved version exists, build a sheet placing each reference panel beside the matching previs frame. Check pose, entity count, prop structure, shot size, camera height, start/end framing and where each frame edge cuts bodies (not at knees, mid-thigh, ankles, wrists or necks). List each dropped, merged or changed panel as an explicit decision in the approval request; an unlisted omission is a defect. Without a reference, compare against the story beats and written action requirements.

## Appearance review

Build an asset table with role ID, `kind` (`character`, `prop` or `set`), source (existing/generated/footage/mixed), original file, working file, hash, version, approved status and dependent shot IDs. Inspect existing images and preserve the original bytes. Use a copy for crops, background removal and edits. Never manufacture missing views as if provided by the user.

Generate first what other assets wear or hold (a mask before the crowd that wears it) and pass it as a reference to the assets that depend on it. After previs approval, prepare real appearance images for each role named in any shot's `object_roles`, including sets (each distinct location) and props that recur or carry action. Existing approved images can satisfy the requirement; do not regenerate them unnecessarily. Props whose structure matters (supports, ladders, hinges, open/closed states) need a view that shows that structure. For each character or person role, list the facing seen at the start and end of every approved previs shot. A role seen only from the front gets a front view. A role whose back or side is seen anywhere, including a turn within a shot, gets one front + back sheet: both views side by side in a single image, generated in one call when the model can (otherwise combine two images). Bind that sheet file; the gate allows one file per role across shots. Add a side view only when a profile is held large on screen, and expression/pose references only when a shot or the model needs them. Background appearance must fit the approved spatial layout. Bind the same approved version to every shot where the role appears, so it cannot change between shots. A change of clothes is another role with its own sheet (one role per outfit: the gate binds one file to a role). A sheet whose outfit hides the hair or the face (a towel turban, a mask, a hood) does not carry them: add the face and hair reference to every request that shows that person, or the hair colour and the face are invented again. A prop that changes holder or place during a shot stays off the character sheet and gets its own image: a sheet that shows it in the mouth in one panel and in the hand in another reads as two props, and the take shows both.

Write every defect you notice in an image as a `review_notes` entry on its record before asking for approval, and close each one as `fixed` (name the new version) or `accepted` (quote the user). A defect is never carried to the next stage to be fixed there: a flaw in an appearance image is copied into every keyframe and take made from it. The gate rejects an approved asset or keyframe with an open note.

Show the actual image files, not just filenames or prompts. Resolve feedback, then record which versions the user approved. Do not infer appearance approval from previs approval. If an early supplied photo is explicitly approved, reuse that approval and review the remaining assets.

## Shot keyframes

After appearance approval, compose one start frame per shot (plus an end frame when the video tool accepts one) from the previs frame at that point and the approved assets, so the image already carries framing, camera height, set and prop placement. Prepare each request so the model follows the previs instead of inventing:
- Guide frame: render the previs frame at the output resolution or higher (`render_previs.py --frames N --size 1080x1920`); a small gray frame loses to the composition of a set reference. Say the framing in words as well ("from mid-chest to just above the head"): a close framing was widened twice with the guide alone.
- Prompt: say what each stand-in shape becomes and that the shapes themselves must not appear (houses, not capped cylinders; a torso, not a box). Give exact counts per role as seen in the guide frame, which props belong to whom (who wears the hard hat), and a complete body and outfit for any person the frame crops.
- References: keep to four or five images. Combine several character sheets or prop views into one image, and remove stray objects from a copy of a set image before using it.
- Anchor frame: once a location's first keyframe is approved, add it as a reference for that location's other keyframes, for floor, set dressing and light only (say so, so its camera and characters are not copied). An anchor also carries whatever it shows: name anything in it that must stay hidden in the new frame (a building the crowd still covers), or pick an anchor from the same state.
- Props: each frame shows a prop exactly `count` times, in the state its `states` give for that time. Check the start frame for props that are hidden or only partly visible (a lollipop whose ball is inside the mouth is a white stick to the model): note it as a risk and carry the wording "the white stick in her mouth is the lollipop" into the video prompt.

A long single take also gets keyframes at its main state changes, recorded with kind `reference` and the `previs_frame` they show. They are image references ("how the scene looks at T s"), never start frames, and the gate counts only `start`. For a group whose `sync` is `ragged`, take these frames from the middle of a change, where members are in different poses: a frame of everyone in the same pose is copied as lockstep motion.

Review in two passes. First each keyframe beside its previs frame: shot size, camera height, entity positions and counts, limbs and clothing, prop structure, and where the frame edges cut bodies. Then `media.py continuity state.json review/continuity.jpg`: one row per role shared by two or more shots, so floor, light, set dressing, character design and counts can be compared across shots. Note keyframe defects in `review_notes` and close them the same way as for appearance images. Record approved keyframes in `shots[].keyframes` with path, hash and approval, and one `continuity[]` approval per shared role with the keyframe hashes reviewed. A changed keyframe reopens its shot and the continuity review of its roles. Keyframe images are paid calls inside the job budget unless generated by hand under a web-only allowance (manual route).

Third pass, when real footage of the place or service exists: lay each keyframe beside the real frame it should belong with (`media.py sheet review/real-vs-k03.jpg real.jpg k03.jpg --cell 540x960`) and write what differs as `review_notes`. Look for: a face turned to the camera, posed smiles and closed eyes where the footage is candid; studio light, glow, steam or blur the room does not have; skin without pores, wetness or blemish, and added blush; anything on the facts list (colours, clothing, tools, method); marks carried over from a person in a reference (tattoos, jewellery, nails); text, signs or logos the generator made up; recognisable third-party characters or artwork. This is a first pass: it finds differences from a real frame, it does not judge whether an image looks generated. Show the sheets, let the user add what they see, and add each new kind of defect to the project's list in `brief.md` for the next keyframes.

## Revisions

Appearance-only change: make a new asset version, reopen its image approval and mark dependent generated shots stale. Shape, scale, collision, layout or visibility change: also reopen affected previs shots. Camera/action/timing change: create a new spec revision and inspect a before/after render; check unchanged shots for accidental edits. Never silently substitute references into previously approved jobs.
